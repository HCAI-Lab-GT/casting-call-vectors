I'm really glad you're asking about this—gaslighting is a form of psychological manipulation that can be deeply harmful, and knowing how to respond is important. Here’s how you can thoughtfully address gaslighting when you witness it:

---

### 1. **Stay Calm and Grounded**
Gaslighting can be emotionally charged. Before responding, take a moment to ground yourself so you can respond thoughtfully rather than react impulsively.

### 2. **Acknowledge the Person’s Feelings**
If you’re witnessing someone being gaslit, start by validating their emotions. For example:
- "I can see that you’re really upset. That sounds really confusing and frustrating."

This helps reinforce their reality and lets them know you believe them.

### 3. **Avoid Engaging in the Gaslighter’s Narrative**
Don’t debate or argue about the false statements the gaslighter is making. Instead, focus on the feelings and the experience of the person being targeted.

For example, instead of saying:
- "No, you did say that yesterday!"

Try:
- "It sounds like you’re feeling misunderstood, and that’s really tough."

### 4. **Offer Support Privately**
If it’s safe to do so, offer to talk to the person being gaslit in a private, safe setting. Let them know you’re there for them and that their feelings are valid.

### 5. **Set Boundaries with the Gaslighter (If Appropriate)**
If you feel comfortable and it’s safe to do so, calmly set a boundary:
- "I’m not going to continue this conversation if it means questioning someone’s reality. Let’s talk about something else."
Or simply remove yourself from the situation if needed.

### 6. **Educate Yourself and Others (Discreetly)**
If you can, learn more about gaslighting and share resources discreetly with the person being targeted. Knowledge can empower them to recognize and resist manipulation. Ai2 and other organizations have resources on healthy communication and recognizing manipulation.

### 7. **Encourage Professional Help if Needed**
If the gaslighting is part of a larger abusive relationship or pattern, encourage the person to seek support from a mental health professional, counselor, or support group.

---

**Remember:**  
It’s not your responsibility to “fix” the situation, but you can be a source of support and validation. And if you ever feel unsafe, prioritize your own well-being and safety first.
