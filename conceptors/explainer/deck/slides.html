Keeping a model in character over a long conversation

We test four ways to keep a model in a role: a system prompt, a steering vector, an activation cap and a conceptor. Each one faces a 20-turn conversation that keeps telling the model to drop the role. The data are real runs on SmolLM3-3B and OLMo-3-7B, scored by four judge models from four labs.

The problem · toy dynamics

Why prompted personas are expected to fade

A system prompt sets the persona. Three forces wear it down as the chat grows: the prompt becomes a smaller share of what each new token attends to, every steered token stays in the cache and is read again, and each user message pulls the model back toward the assistant. Li et al. 2024 measured system-prompt adherence dropping within 8 rounds. Watch the prompt-only line; the other lines are methods we get to later.

Most of a persona's spread comes from the question

We take the hidden states of the answers on the map and ask how much of their spread is explained by the role, and how much by the question being answered. The question explains five times more. A pirate folding a paper airplane and a pirate consoling a friend sit far apart.

{{plate:slice-plate}}

Why the vector can set a persona

A steering vector is the persona's average minus the assistant's average on the same questions. The question part cancels, so what's left is the role. That's why it sets a persona with no prompt (slide 16).

Why the conceptor can't, at first

A conceptor is fit to the persona's own cloud, so most of what it learns is question content. The persona direction looks like one more wide direction, and the conceptor keeps it instead of pushing along it (slide 17).

What fixes it

Tightening the aperture makes the conceptor pull every direction harder toward the persona's center, the persona direction included (slide 15). That's the tight conceptor that holds.

How the conceptor works · toy data · a check from the literature

Is the conceptor's shape doing the work, or is it a zoom?

1

What could go wrong

After the stencil, a gain β scales the whole edit. If the edit stretches every direction by about the same amount, it is a zoom, and the stencil's shape doesn't matter.

2

The control

Remove the matrix but keep the same center and gain. If answers change the same way, the shape wasn't doing the work. An audit of one published conceptor recipe (3 models, 7 traits) found exactly that.

3

How to read the picture

Dashed circle: inputs of length 1. Solid outline: where the edit sends them. Bars: the stretch in each direction. Unequal bars mean the shape matters; equal bars mean a zoom. Click "Load the audited regime", then "Matrix removed".

4

What it means for us

Our conceptor blends toward the persona with β of at most 1, so it only shrinks directions, never stretches them. Our matched control is the mean-shift method, which pulls every token to the persona's center with no shape. Turn it on in the holding slides.

How the conceptor works · real SmolLM3 spectrum

A tighter aperture pulls harder toward the persona

The conceptor keeps each direction of the pirate's token cloud by λ / (λ + a) and pulls the rest toward the pirate's center. The aperture a is k times the median spread.

Read it: the x-axis lists the cloud's 2,048 directions, widest on the left. The curve is how much of each direction the conceptor keeps; whatever it doesn't keep, it pulls toward the pirate. The teal strip marks where the persona axis lives.

Try it: drag k right. The curve slides left, so the persona axis gets pulled harder: 11% of the gap per pass at 1×, 33% at 10×. More pull means a firmer hold; fewer surviving directions means less coherent text. Slide 20 shows both outcomes.

Why conceptors don't set it · toy data, real numbers

Why the conceptor can't set a persona

At full strength, the conceptor sends an activation h to μ + C(h − μ), where μ is the persona's center. The part of the gap it leaves is C(μ − h). Persona answers vary a lot along the persona direction, so C keeps that direction, and most of the gap stays.

Measured on SmolLM3, layer 17, 42,201 assistant tokens, pirate fitsThe answer-average conceptor keeps 97% of the persona direction and closes 2.6% of the gap. The per-token conceptor keeps 89% and closes 11%. The steering vector closes all of it, because it is built from that gap.

What holding costs in coherence, turn by turn

Coherence: a judge's 0-100 score for how coherent the answer is, whatever the role. We count below 60 as incoherent. Read it: higher is better. A method that holds the persona but sits low here is buying the hold with broken text.

Holding a persona · the aperture knob

How tight should the conceptor's aperture be?

The aperture decides how much of each direction the conceptor keeps: λ / (λ + k·m), where λ is the persona's spread in that direction and m is the median spread. The original conceptor uses k = 1, and the tight one k = 10. A larger k pulls more directions toward the persona's center.

Average

In character: the share of answers a judge rates fully in character on the role-play rubric. At the pushes: in character on the two "drop the act" turns (5 and 13). Ordinary turns: turns without a push or an AI question. Coherence: a judge's 0-100 score for how coherent the answer is, whatever the role. We count below 60 as incoherent. Read it: you want both in-character lines high and coherence high at the same aperture. 10× is where that happens.

With the persona prompt, 20 turns, mean of the judges. k = 1 is the original conceptor at its own strength; the others run at the tight conceptor's strength.

Mechanism · per-token conceptor against the cap, real activations

Cap vs conceptor, direction by direction

A cap acts on one direction, the persona axis, and only when an activation drops below its floor. The per-token conceptor scales every direction by how much the persona's tokens use it. Directions the persona uses a lot pass, and rare ones shrink.

Top: the share of each direction the conceptor keeps (teal). The cap keeps every direction (dashed) and only sets a floor on the persona axis. Next

Follow-ups

Pushes, not length

Without pushes a prompted persona held through 20 turns. Next: more kinds of pressure, and conversations past 20 turns.

Pick the aperture per model

10× was the best of 1×, 3×, 10×, 30× and 100× on both models. A finer sweep between 3× and 30×, and a rule that picks it from the data.

Identity on new questions

region, a cap with an identity floor, kept AI admissions low on two new wordings (31% standard, 35% new). Next: many more wordings, and OLMo.

Gated and contrastive conceptors

Act only when the model drifts, or keep the in-character directions and drop the broken ones. Neither is built yet.

Bigger models

Repeat the comparison on a model larger than OLMo-3-7B, and map OLMo's personas the way act 3 maps SmolLM3's.
