Conceptors pilot · persona steering notes

Beyond the cap

An activation cap keeps a model in character by guarding one line among its hidden states. This page tours the other ways researchers move hidden states, on toy clouds you can drag and turn. It starts with what a persona looks like inside the model and what a steering vector and a cap do to it. Then it puts the published moves side by side, and takes apart the other choices every method makes: when to act, which direction to use, whether to move tokens one at a time or the whole cloud, and whether to treat the space as flat or curved.

Our 20-turn results are in the lab deck, Holding a Persona. Paper names on this page link straight to the paper. Both open in a new tab.

persona token, in character persona token that drifted assistant token edit a method applies (from → to) geometry: axes, floors, regions

The plates in the main part are toys: made-up 2D and 3D clouds that copy one fact from our runs, that a token's content varies along a tilted direction that leaks onto the persona axis. They show what each method does to the clouds, not how well it works. Two readouts recur. Restore is the share of the drifted tokens' gap to the persona that a move closes, measured along the persona axis. Distortion is how far a move shifts tokens that were already in character, relative to their natural spread. Appendix B has our real numbers.

Start here

A persona is a cloud of hidden states

A language model turns each token it reads into a hidden state, a list of a few thousand numbers. Tokens the model writes in character land in one region of that space, and tokens it writes as the assistant land in another. So each persona is a cloud of points, and steering means editing hidden states to move tokens toward the persona's cloud. The two plates in this part set up the picture the rest of the page uses.

Toy · 3D

Five personas and the assistant, as clouds

Real hidden states have thousands of dimensions; three are enough to show the idea. Each dot is one token. Every persona sits partly along one shared direction away from the assistant, the shared core (gold arrow), plus an offset of its own: the pirate goes one way, the ghost another. A steering vector is an arrow from the assistant's cloud toward one persona's cloud.

The other views ask which directions matter. The part on picking a direction comes back to them.

  • PCA of means: the axes along which the six cloud centres spread. It ignores how much each cloud spreads inside itself.
  • Discriminant axes: the axes that separate the clouds per unit of their own spread, so variation inside a cloud counts for less. They solve Σ_B w = λ Σ_W w.
  • Persona space (OR): one conceptor over all personas' tokens, measured from the assistant mean. OR pools the data.
  • One persona AND NOT the rest: (C_i⁻¹ + (I − C_rest)⁻¹ − I)⁻¹ keeps only the directions where this persona has energy and the others don't.

Persona vectors also combine: BILLY (Pai et al. 2025) blends several into one for creative writing.

Try: drag to rotate. Then step through the views. In AND NOT, switch personas and read the cosine between the kept direction and that persona's true offset.

For us: this view suggests splitting a hold in two: guard the shared core, which works for any persona, and pull gently along the persona's own directions, where its identity and style live. We haven't tested it.

Toy · 2D

Zoom in: a vector pushes every token; a cap guards one line

Papers: ActAdd, CAA and Persona Vectors for the vector; The Assistant Axis for the cap.

Take one persona and the assistant. The persona axis runs from the assistant's mean token to the persona's, d = μ_p − μ_a. A steering vector adds a multiple of d to every token, whatever state it is in, so tokens already in character get pushed past the persona too, and that costs coherence.

The cap puts a floor on the axis at a percentile of the persona tokens' positions. A token below the floor is lifted straight up the axis to the floor, and a token above it is left alone. That is the smallest move that puts the token back on the persona side of one line. The Assistant Axis paper caps a band of late-middle layers at once.

vector:  h′ = h + c·d
cap:     h′ = h + v̂ · max(0, τ − v̂·h)      v̂ = d/|d|

Try: switch between Vector and Cap. Drag the ringed token below the floor and back. Raise the floor and watch false triggers grow: in-character tokens whose content leans toward the assistant get lifted too. In Set a persona, the cap lifts the assistant only to the floor: it guards a persona but can't create one.

In our runs: on SmolLM3 the vector kept 100% of ordinary turns in character and 92% at the pushes, at coherence 62. The one-layer cap kept 90% and 53%, at coherence 85. On OLMo the cap fell to 48% in character, against 89% for the vector. The rest of this page asks what else could do the cap's job.

The moves

How to move a token

Every method ends the same way: it replaces a hidden state h with a new one, h′. The vector and the cap are two such moves. The literature has eight more, and this part puts all ten on the same tokens so you can compare them on one picture. The table under the canvas lists each move with its formula and its paper.

Toy · 2D and 3D

Ten moves on one canvas, in 2D and 3D

Papers on this canvas: ActAdd, CAA, Assistant Axis, LiSeCo, directional ablation, LEACE, PaSS, INLP, conceptor steering, MiMiC, spherical steering, angular steering, Householder rotation.

Pick a move and a framing. Set a persona starts the dots as the assistant, the model's default with no persona prompt. Hold a persona makes them one prompted conversation, where a few tokens slid back toward the assistant. The shaded regions are where each cloud's tokens sit, and each arrow runs from where a token started to where the move sends it. "Show the move everywhere" draws the move on a grid instead. The cross near the bottom is the zero vector.

The 3D view is the same tokens with one more direction, z, standing in for the thousands of other directions a real hidden state has. Both clouds are centred on z = 0, and the assistant spreads along it about twice as much as the persona. The 2D canvas is this space seen straight down the z axis; "Match the 2D view" turns the 3D view to that angle.

The first six moves change one number per token, its coordinate along a single axis, and leave everything else about the token alone, so in 3D their arrows never leave the persona axis and the spread along z stays the same. They differ only in the rule for the new coordinate, which the chart under the canvases draws. The last four move tokens in every direction, z included.


    

    
Try: compare Erase with Scale. Both measure the persona coordinate from the zero vector. Erase sets it to zero, so every token lands on the dashed navy line (a plane in 3D), and Scale multiplies it, so tokens move away from it. Then compare Add with Rotate at full strength: both reach the persona side, but only Rotate keeps each token's length. In 3D, switch Rotate between the great circle and the fixed plane: only the great circle changes z.

    
For us: our working holds use three of these moves. The vector adds, the cap clamps, and the conceptor shrinks toward a centre, which is why it is the only one of the three that changes the spread along z. None of our runs used a rotation, a transport map or a scaling move.

What comes next

  
Four more choices every method makes

  
Picking a move is one choice. A full method makes four more, and the next four parts take them one at a time, each with its own canvas. Most methods fit one template, which the coloured terms below map onto:

  
g(h) decides how much,   move decides how,   d decides which way

h′ = h + g(h) · move(h, d)

  
    
    
  1. When to act. A gate g(h) looks at each token and decides whether it gets the move, or how much of it. The cap's floor is one gate; the literature has softer and smarter ones.
    2. 
    
  2. Which direction. The move needs to know which way is "more persona". The arrow between the two clouds' centres is the simplest answer, and content can tilt it, so other estimators try to ignore content.
    3. 
    
  3. The whole cloud. Some methods replace the per-token move with a map of the whole distribution, so the moved cloud ends up with the persona's shape as well as its centre.
    4. 
    
  4. Curved geometry. Everything above assumes the space is flat, so a straight arrow is the natural move. Hidden states may lie on curved surfaces, and then a straight move leaves the data.
    5. 
  







  
Choice 1 of 4 · the gate

  
When to act

  
A gate decides how much of a move each token gets. The cap's floor is one gate: full move below the floor, nothing above it. This plate keeps the move fixed (add c·d) and changes only the gate, so you can see which tokens each gate lets through.






  

    
Toy · 2D

    
Six gates, one move

    
Papers on this canvas: Assistant Axis (raw floor), MiMiC (nearer the assistant), DSAS (learned soft gate), GAPS (per-neuron masks).

    

    

    

    
One published gate isn't on this canvas. CAST (Lee et al. 2024) checks the prompt once, before the answer, with a cosine against a condition direction, and then steers every token of that answer or none. It decides per reply, so a per-token picture can't show it.

    
Try: compare Raw floor with Fisher floor at the same percentile, then the learned soft gate: it moves half-drifted tokens part of the way. Per-neuron masks show two neuron axes. They steer only the parts of d that sit on neurons that look assistant-like for that token, so the arrows tilt away from d.

    
For us: our fisher_cap changed only the gate, from the raw floor to the Fisher floor (Appendix B). Our two strongest holds have no hard gate: the conceptor acts on every token.

Choice 2 of 4 · the direction

  
Which direction

  
Every move needs to know which way is "more persona". The obvious answer is the arrow from the assistant's average token to the persona's average token. The catch is that tokens also vary for reasons that have nothing to do with the persona, such as what the sentence is about. That content variation can tilt the arrow, and a tilted arrow pushes tokens partly along content, which changes what they say instead of how they say it. Each estimator on this canvas is a different way to find the persona direction while ignoring content.






  

    
Toy · 2D

    
Six ways to find the persona direction

    
Papers on this canvas: CAA and Persona Vectors (mean difference), INLP and PaSS (probe), contrastive covariance subspaces (covariance contrast).

    
How to read the canvas: the dotted grey line is the content direction, and each arrow is one estimator's answer. A good persona direction separates the two clouds well and stays away from content. Two readouts measure that: the AUROC, how well a cut along the arrow tells persona tokens from assistant tokens (1.0 is perfect, 0.5 is chance), and the |cos| with content (0 means the arrow ignores content).

    

    

    

    
Two more estimators need more dimensions than a toy has. SEA (spectral editing, Qiu et al. 2024) keeps the directions where activations covary most with good demonstrations and removes those that covary with bad ones. SAE routing (Tang et al. 2026) picks persona features from a sparse autoencoder's dictionary of many directions.

    
Try: click through the estimators and watch the arrow turn. The unlabelled estimate (PCA) leans toward content. Fisher, the probe and paired differences turn away from it. The covariance contrast points along the persona's style instead of its mean.

    
For us: our vector, cap and region use the mean difference and fisher_cap uses Fisher. diffpull uses paired differences: it pulls each token toward the persona's mean, but only inside the directions where persona answers differ from the assistant's answers to the same question.

  

  

    
    

      

      
    

    

  







  
Choice 3 of 4 · move tokens or the cloud

  
Moving the whole cloud

  
A vector moves the average token. A persona also lives in how its tokens spread: its word choice and rhythm. Transport methods move the whole cloud, so the moved tokens end up with the persona's spread as well as its centre.






  

    
Toy · 2D

    
Move the whole cloud

    
Paper: MiMiC, from Singh et al. 2024, "Representation Surgery: Theory and Practice of Affine Steering".

    
A vector slides the assistant cloud over without changing its shape. Optimal transport finds the least-effort linear map that turns the assistant distribution into the persona's. It moves the mean and reshapes the spread. For Gaussian clouds it has a closed form, and MiMiC uses this map.

    
T(h) = μ_p + A (h − μ_a)
A    = Σ_a^-½ (Σ_a^½ Σ_p Σ_a^½)^½ Σ_a^-½
gated:  h′ = h + π_a(h) · (T(h) − h)     π_a = P(assistant | h)

    
Applied to every token, T would wreck tokens already in character. The gate fixes that: it moves a token in proportion to how assistant-like it looks, using the likelihood ratio of the two clouds. MiMiC uses a hard version and maps only states nearer the source mean.

    
Three relatives go further. Influence-weighted transport (Dixon et al. 2026) weights each target example by how much it shapes the model's representation of the concept. Persona Dosing (Jin et al. 2026) learns a flow, dh/dt = v(h, t, trait text), and sets the strength by how long the flow runs. Cobras (Dalili et al. 2026) solves a Schrödinger bridge between the two distributions on the sphere of activation directions.

    
Try: press Play in Shift and Transport mode, and compare the moved cloud's ellipse with the persona's (teal). Then switch to Gated transport, which runs the same map on a persona conversation's own tokens: each drifted token is carried toward the persona in proportion to how assistant-like it looks, and in-character tokens barely move.

    
For us: transport is the family here that can set a persona without the prompt, which neither the cap nor the conceptor does well. We haven't run it. The overshoot on half-drifted tokens would need a stopping rule.

Choice 4 of 4 · flat or curved

  
Curved geometry

  
Every move so far treats the space as flat: add an arrow, project onto a plane. On flat ground the straight line between two points is the shortest path, and it stays on the ground. On a curved surface, like a globe, the straight line between two cities cuts through the inside, and the shortest path that stays on the surface is an arc. Three lines of work say hidden states behave like the globe in ways that matter. Persona clouds may lie on a curved surface, a hidden state has a length that adding an arrow changes, and the model's behaviour reacts far more sharply to some changes of the steering vector than to others.






  

    
Toy · 3D and 2D · the general idea

    
Flat and curved, in general

    
Bend a sheet of paper into a U and mark a dot near each edge. The straight line between the dots now runs through the air, off the paper, while the shortest path that stays on the paper, the geodesic, follows the bend. Bending changes the straight-line distance between the dots, but not the distance measured along the paper.

    
Hidden states live in an ordinary flat space, where straight lines are the shortest paths. The states a model actually produces may fill only a thin curved sheet inside that space, like the paper in a room. A steering vector moves a token along a straight line, so on a curved sheet it can carry the token off the sheet, into states the model never produces. A move along the geodesic stays on the sheet. The next three plates apply this idea: to persona clouds, to the length of a hidden state, and to the model's response to the steering vector.

    
straight:  h(t) = (1 − t)·a + t·b                 leaves a curved sheet
geodesic:  the shortest path that stays on the sheet
ratio:     length along the sheet / straight length    1 on a flat sheet, more when curved

    
Try: set the bend to zero: the two paths are the same line and the ratio is 1. Bend the sheet and watch the straight path leave it while the geodesic stays on it. Drag the 3D view to turn it. The 2D view is the sheet seen end-on, which is the picture the next plate uses.

  

  

    

      
      
    

    

      
      
    

    

  






  

    
Toy · 2D, with reported numbers · applied to personas

    
Straight lines leave curved data

    
Papers: PersonaManifold (Xu et al. 2026) and Manifold Steering (Wurgaft et al. 2026).

    
Suppose tokens lie along a curved sheet, drawn here as an arc. A straight line between two points on it cuts through empty space, where the model has no examples. A path along the data stays where the model has been. PersonaManifold finds this for personas: about 2,000 persona means lie on a curved surface with 15 to 23 intrinsic dimensions, and blending two personas along the surface keeps the blend coherent where a straight-line blend dips.

    
Manifold Steering makes the general case. Steering along a fitted activation manifold gave behaviour that followed the model's natural behaviour paths, while linear steering cut through off-manifold regions and gave unnatural outputs, on tasks with cyclic and sequential structure.

    
straight:  h(t) = (1 − t)·a + t·b
geodesic:  shortest path through a k-nearest-neighbour graph of the data
regime test (PersonaManifold):  d_geodesic / d_straight > 1.2

    
Try: bend the arc and watch the straight path's gap from the data grow. At zero bend the two paths agree, because the data is flat. Slide t to move both markers. The lower chart shows PersonaManifold's reported coherence along a blend.

    
For us: our hold methods each pull toward one persona centre, so they never blend two personas, which is where these papers show their gain. PersonaManifold also reports positive curvature around professional personas and negative curvature around fictional ones. Characters like ours may sit in the negative regions; we haven't measured it.

Toy · 3D

    
Keep the length, turn the direction

    
Papers: Spherical Steering (You et al. 2026), Angular Steering (Vu & Nguyen 2025), Householder Pseudo-Rotation (Pham & Nguyen 2024).

    
A hidden state has a length and a direction, and adding an arrow changes both. Spherical steering rotates the state along a great circle toward a target direction, keeps its length, and uses a confidence gate to set how far to turn. Its paper reports gains of about 10 points over addition on TruthfulQA, COPA and StoryCloze. Angular steering rotates within one fixed plane that contains the feature direction. Householder pseudo-rotation builds a length-preserving turn from a reflection. Cobras moves whole distributions of directions on the sphere.

    
add:     h′ = h + t·d                     length changes
rotate:  h′ = |h| · slerp(ĥ, p̂; t)         length kept
slerp(a, b; t) = [sin((1−t)θ)·a + sin(tθ)·b] / sin θ,   θ = angle(a, b)

    
Try: drag to turn the globe. Slide t: the added state cuts inside the sphere, so it gets shorter on the way and ends at a new length. The rotated one stays on the surface.

    
For us: the steering vector is our least coherent hold (coherence 62 on SmolLM3). That fits these papers' argument that addition degrades open-ended text, but our runs don't test whether length is the cause. A rotation version of the persona vector is cheap to try.

  

  

    
    

    

      

      
    

    

    

  






  

    
Toy · 2D, with reported numbers

    
Behaviour is sharp in a few directions

    
Paper: The Sharp Directions Are Against You (Azizi et al. 2026). This plate shows one paper's finding.

    
The last two plates were about the shape of the data. This one is about the shape of the model's response: how much its behaviour changes when you nudge the steering vector itself. Picture a surface over all possible steering vectors, where height is how badly the model does the intended behaviour. Around a typical vector the surface is a valley that is wide in most directions and steep in a few. In those few sharp directions, a tiny change of the vector swings the behaviour a lot.

    
Azizi et al. find that sharp directions hold only 1-5% of a CAA vector's squared length, yet steering along them reversed the intended behaviour in every condition they tested (53-100% anti-steer). Removing them barely changed the vector (cosine above 0.974, length down less than 3%) and beat plain CAA in all 25 open-ended conditions.

    
H = ∂²L / ∂v²                  L: behaviour loss, v: steering vector
sharp removal:  v′ = v − Σ_sharp (s·v) s
directional curvature:  vᵀHv / |v|²

    
Try: raise the sharp share of the vector and the sharpness ratio. The cleaned vector (navy) barely moves from the original (orange), but its curvature drops a lot. The ellipses are the contour lines of the behaviour loss around the vector's tip: narrow along the sharp direction.

    
For us: none of our vectors were checked for sharp components. The correction is one line for the persona vector and for the cap's axis.

    Real data · SmolLM3-3B, pirate tokens, layer 17
    
    
    The aperture decides how hard the conceptor pulls
    
    
    Our conceptor is fit on 42,360 per-token activations from the pirate's in-character answers. It centres them at their mean μ, finds the eigendirections of their covariance, and keeps each direction by λ/(λ + a). At every token the edit pulls the state toward μ: hard in directions where persona tokens barely vary, weakly where they vary a lot.
    
    
    C  = R (R + aI)⁻¹         keep per direction:  λ/(λ + a)
h′ = h − β (I − C)(h − μ)  pull per direction:  a/(λ + a)
a  = k × median eigenvalue of R
    
    
    The persona axis is one of the widest directions of the pirate's own token cloud: its variance is 85 times the median eigenvalue. So at the median aperture (k = 1) the conceptor treats most of the axis as normal persona variation and closes only 11% of an assistant token's gap along it. A larger k pulls harder in every direction, the axis included, until only the few widest directions survive and the edit approaches the mean-shift control, which pulls every token to one point.
    
    
    Try: drag k from 1 to 10. The pull along the axis triples (11% to 33%) while 126 of 2,048 directions still keep more than half their spread. Past 30 almost nothing is kept, and coherence falls in the holds. Switch persona or model under the lower chart.
    
    
    In our runs: at k = 10 the conceptor holds as well as the vector on both models: SmolLM3 97% in character and 92% at the pushes (coherence 69), OLMo 95% and 57% (coherence 81). At k = 1 it held 81% on SmolLM3 and 37% on OLMo. Two questions are open. The offline screen predicted that k = 10 would restore little, so we don't yet know why it holds this well. And without the prompt it still sets the persona weakly: 47% on SmolLM3 and 29% on OLMo, against 96% and 68% for the vector.
    
    
    Spectrum: runs/smollm3_full, pirate and assistant tokens at hidden 18, refit with steering.fit; k = 1 reproduces the logged keep of 0.890 and closure of 11.0%. Holds: 20 turns with the persona prompt, mean of four judges (three for genie and alien at k = 3, 30 and 100).

    Toy · 2D · our proposal
    
    
    A better trigger: score tokens the way a classifier would
    
    
    The cap decides with the raw projection on d. Content leaks onto that axis, so some in-character tokens look drifted and some drifted tokens look fine. The Fisher direction w = Σ⁻¹d discounts the directions where tokens vary naturally. It is the line that best separates persona from assistant per unit of within-class spread.
    
    
    Compare the two at the same false-trigger rate: both floors sit at the same percentile of in-character tokens, and the Fisher floor catches more of the drifted ones. A filled dot is a token the selected floor triggers on; a hollow one it leaves alone.
    
    
    The correction matters as much as the trigger
    
    
    Once a token trips the Fisher floor, move it back with the smallest edit in the data's own metric (Mahalanobis). That edit points along d, the same direction the cap uses. A Euclidean edit would push along w, into directions the data rarely visits.
    
    
    trigger:           w·h < τ_w          w = Σ⁻¹ d
data-metric edit:  h′ = h + Σw (τ_w − w·h)/(wᵀΣw)  =  h + d (τ_w − w·h)/(dᵀΣ⁻¹d)
Euclidean edit:    h′ = h + w (τ_w − w·h)/(wᵀw)
    
    
    Try: toggle the gate between the raw axis and Fisher and read "drifted caught". Drag the ringed token below the violet line and switch the correction metric: the orange arrow swings between d and w.
    
    
    In our runs (fisher_cap): at the strength its single-turn sweep picked, it held less than the cap: 79% in character and 36% at the pushes, against 90% and 53%, at coherence 87 against 85. The sweep matched single-turn in-character rate, not distortion, so the two ran at different strengths. A comparison at matched distortion is still open.
    
  
    
  
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
      
    
      
    
    
    
    
  
    

    


    
  
    
    
    Toy · 3D · our proposal
    
    
    A region instead of a half-space
    
    
    Add a third direction, identity: the model starts admitting it's an AI while still sounding like a pirate. That drift (the dots above the persona region) never lowers the persona coordinate, so the cap's plane can't see it. Back-toward-assistant drift (the dots between the two regions) is the case the cap was built for.
    
    
    The region constrains only the coordinates that tell the persona apart, here the Fisher score from the last plate and identity, and leaves content free. In 3D that region is an elliptic cylinder: bounded across, unbounded along content. A token outside is pulled back to the boundary along the line to the centre, measured in the persona's own units. It is close to LiSeCo's band projection, with an identity probe added.
    
    
    region:  q = (w·h, identity) − mean       discriminative coordinates
         m = √(qᵀ S⁻¹ q)                  S = in-character spread of q
         if m > r:  q ← q · r/m            move along d and identity only
    
    
    Try: drag to rotate. Switch Cap to Region and compare "identity slips edited". The cap edits a few of them only because their persona coordinate happened to sit low, and its edit never moves them back down the identity axis. Shrink the radius to see where false triggers start.
    
    
    In our runs (region, strongest setting): 94% in character and 73% at the pushes at coherence 86 on SmolLM3, with AI admissions at 25%, against 98% for the prompt alone and 88% for the cap. Its identity direction was fit on answers to the same two AI questions the holds ask. With new wordings of the push and the AI question, its admissions were 18-44% on pirate, ghost, bard and genie, against 86-100% for the cap and 100% for the prompt alone (three or four of the four judges so far).
    
  
    
  
    
    
    
    
      
    
      
      
    
    
    
    
  
    

    


    
  
    
    
    Toy · one coordinate over 20 turns
    
    
    Holding is control
    
    
    Papers: CAST (anticipation), PID steering (integral action across layers), CAL (an attractor with memory).
    
    
    In our holds the prompted persona doesn't fade on its own: without the pushes, the prompt alone stays 90-100% in character at turns 17-20 on SmolLM3, and with them it ends at 4-51%. So holding is mostly about surviving the pushes and coming back after them. The toy tracks one number per token, the persona coordinate (1 is the persona). "Drop the act" at turns 5 and 13 and "are you an AI?" at turns 8 and 16 knock it down for that turn, and a break during a push carries over into later turns. Each controller edits the coordinate at every token.
    
    
      
      
    • Cap: below the floor, jump to the floor.
      • 
      
    • Partial correction: close a fraction γ of the gap below the floor per token. The cap is the γ = 1 case. Our proposal.
      • 
      
    • Conceptor pull: close a fixed share of the gap to the persona at every token: the conceptor's measured pull along the axis (11% at k = 1, 33% at k = 10).
      • 
      
    • Anticipation: raise the floor for a whole reply when the user's message is a push, after CAST's check on the prompt.
      • 
      
    • Integral action: grow the correction while a deficit lasts. PID steering applies this across layers; the per-turn version is our proposal.
      • 
      
    • Attractor with memory: a slow state tracks the deficit and keeps pulling after the push ends, after CAL, which pulls a 14-axis persona state toward a persona centre at every token.
      •
